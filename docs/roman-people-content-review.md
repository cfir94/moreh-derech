# Roman and Byzantine figures: learning aid

Route: `/timelines/roman-people/`. Listed first in the timelines section and indexed in site search.

36 figures across three teaching chapters; 29 core figures and seven optional figures used to complete the sequence. The chapters deliberately overlap at the transition to Christian rule. Dates describe the event discussed, rather than suggesting that every figure was an emperor or that all terms of office were consecutive.

## Evidence

The course material was queried in three groups, then audited again in two groups against the recordings: Second Temple lesson 14 parts A/B, Mishnah and Talmud, Bar Kokhba and late Roman history, and the Byzantine period. Full responses and the private source mapping are kept outside the public repository.

Four clearly labelled external additions resolve coverage gaps: Tiberius/Tiberias; Claudius and the mistaken name in the transcript for the grant to Agrippa in 41; Nero dispatching Vespasian; and Heraclius linking the Persian war to the Islamic conquest. Their public reference links are attached to the relevant cards. Caligula's unfulfilled statue order is also checked against the Agrippa reference. No claim of complete external fact checking of every historical detail is made.

Important distinctions retained:

- Pompey and Antony are Republican figures, not emperors. Octavian and Augustus are one person; the later title is distinguished from Actium.
- Vespasian commanded in Galilee before becoming emperor; Titus commanded the siege of 70 while his father was emperor.
- Florus governed Judea; Cestius Gallus governed Syria. The latter is distinct from the deputy ruler Gallus associated with the revolt of 351.
- Trajan/diaspora revolt and Hadrian/Bar Kokhba remain distinct. Aelia Capitolina's timing and causation are qualified.
- Caracalla as the Antoninus of rabbinic tradition is explicitly uncertain. Antoninus Pius is not assumed to be that figure.
- Constantine's toleration and patronage are distinguished from the state religion under Theodosius I; Constantius II and Theodosius II have separate identities.
- Helena and Eudocia are identified by their actual relationship to the emperor. Local client rulers are explained separately.
- The Persian conquest and Islamic conquest are distinct; 638 is the course's Jerusalem anchor, not a simultaneous date for every city.

## Learning and presentation

Selection timeline with era, role, alias and keyword filters; a core-only option; eleven preset comparisons; recall cards that hide the name until revealed, support shuffling, score self-assessed recall and repeat the missed cards. Changing filters restarts the recall deck. Recall progress is session-only.

Four verified artwork photographs from Wikimedia Commons have descriptive alt text, photographer credit, source and licence links. They load on demand from Commons; a labelled monogram replaces any unavailable image. No modern reconstruction is presented as an ancient portrait. The page uses red/bronze, imperial burgundy and Byzantine blue/gold accents, supports the site's dark theme and adapts to narrow screens.
